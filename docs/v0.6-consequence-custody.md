# CAGE v0.6 — Consequence Custody

## Purpose

CAGE v0.6 introduces **Consequence Custody**: the controlled boundary between
a CAGE Decision and a real-world external mutation.

v0.5 established how CAGE represents an intended business Consequence, evaluates
it, and records the resulting Decision.

v0.6 addresses the next question:

> Once a Consequence is permitted, how can it cross into external execution
> without losing control of authority, identity, or evidence about what
> actually happened?

The central architectural rule remains:

```text
Decision != Effect
```

An `ADMITTED` Decision means CAGE permits the operation to proceed toward
execution.

It does not mean the operation was executed.

An execution attempt also does not prove that the intended business consequence
became effective.

Likewise, an execution failure does not prove `NO_BIND`.

CAGE establishes external outcome separately through authoritative
verification before creating an Effect claim.

For the terminology used here, see
[Core Terminology](architecture.md#core-terminology).

---

## Core Principle

A Decision can make a Consequence eligible for custody.

Custody is the point where that permission is carried toward external
execution under controlled conditions.

The v0.6 lineage is:

```text
Action
  |
  v
Consequence
  |
  v
Attempt
  |
  v
Evaluation
  |
  v
Decision
  |
  v
DecisionProof
  |
  v
-----------------------------
     CONSEQUENCE CUSTODY
-----------------------------
  |
  v
ExecutionAttempt
  |
  v
EffectAdapter
  |
  v
External System
  |
  v
AdapterExecutionResult
  |
  v
EffectVerifier
  |
  v
EffectVerificationResult
  |
  v
Effect
  |
  v
EffectProof
  |
  v
Warrant
```

The custody boundary keeps two different facts separate:

```text
permission to attempt execution
```

and:

```text
evidence of what became effective
```

A Decision provides the first.

Authoritative verification provides the basis for the second.

---

## v0.6 Implemented Scope

v0.6 adds the generic custody and verification contracts needed to carry a
permitted Consequence across the execution boundary.

The release includes:

- `ExecutionAttempt` identity;
- custody eligibility;
- exact effect selection for `ADMITTED` and `NARROWED`;
- scoped `ExecutionCapability`;
- explicit custody execution;
- request-level adapter observations;
- authoritative Effect verification;
- explicit uncertainty;
- reconciliation without re-execution;
- stronger `NO_BIND` semantics;
- verification-backed `EffectProof`;
- execution and verification lineage in the Warrant; and
- predecessor-Warrant lineage.

These contracts remain vendor-neutral.

The Core does not depend on AWS, Azure, GCP, or another provider-specific
execution model.

---

## Consequence Custody Boundary

Custody begins after CAGE has produced a Decision.

It works with the existing evaluation lineage rather than recreating it.

Conceptually:

```text
Consequence
    +
Decision
    |
    v
Custody
```

The Decision answers whether the Consequence may proceed toward execution.

Custody then applies the execution-specific controls needed to cross that
boundary.

For example, an `ADMITTED` Decision cannot be converted directly into:

```text
BOUND
```

`BOUND` is an Effect state and requires authoritative verification.

The execution path therefore remains separate from the Effect-establishment
path.

---

## Execution Is Explicit

Evaluation never performs an external mutation by itself.

This flow is not part of CAGE:

```text
evaluate_attempt()
    |
    v
ADMITTED
    |
    v
automatic external execution
```

Instead, execution requires its own explicit lifecycle step:

```text
Decision
    |
    v
ExecutionAttempt
    |
    v
execute_under_custody()
```

This keeps evaluation, replay, execution, reconciliation, and proof
construction as separate operations.

A caller can therefore reason about each stage independently rather than
treating admission as an implicit side effect.

---

## ExecutionAttempt Identity

`ExecutionAttempt` is different from the evaluation `Attempt`.

An evaluation Attempt represents:

> one evaluation of a Consequence

An `ExecutionAttempt` represents:

> one identified attempt to carry an eligible Decision into external execution

The current lineage is:

```text
execution_attempt_id
decision
optional previous_execution_attempt_id
```

The Consequence is reached through the Decision lineage.

Therefore:

```text
evaluation Attempt identity
!=
ExecutionAttempt identity
```

This distinction matters because reevaluation and external execution are
different events.

A replay creates another evaluation Attempt.

An explicitly supported execution retry would create another
`ExecutionAttempt` while keeping the same Consequence.

v0.6 defines the identity model needed for that distinction, but does not add a
general execution-retry orchestration feature.

---

## Custody Eligibility

Custody decides whether a Decision can reach the adapter and which effect may
be executed.

`select_effect_for_custody()` follows this mapping:

```text
ADMITTED
    -> original requested effect

NARROWED
    -> Decision.permitted_effect

HELD
ESCALATED
REFUSED
    -> custody ineligible
```

For `ADMITTED`, custody uses the original requested effect.

For `NARROWED`, custody uses only the explicitly permitted effect.

`HELD`, `ESCALATED`, and `REFUSED` stop before adapter invocation.

This preserves the meaning of the Decision at the execution boundary.

In particular, the broader original request from a `NARROWED` Decision must
never be sent to the adapter.

---

## Scoped Execution Capability

v0.6 represents execution authority with `ExecutionCapability`.

Its current minimal fields are:

```text
capability_id
consequence_id
action_type
resource_id
```

Before execution, `validate_capability_for_custody()` checks that the
capability matches:

```text
same consequence_id
same action_type
same resource_id
```

A capability issued for another Consequence, action type, or Resource cannot
be reused for the current execution.

This keeps the authority presented to custody scoped to the business operation
being executed.

`ExecutionCapability` is not a credential container.

It does not hold:

- OAuth tokens;
- API keys;
- passwords;
- provider credentials;
- AWS role ARNs;
- Azure tokens;
- IAM policies;
- secrets; or
- permission documents.

CAGE models the scope of execution authority relevant to the Consequence.

Credential acquisition, storage, and provider-specific authorization remain
outside the generic Core.

---

## EffectAdapter

`EffectAdapter` is the vendor-neutral boundary used to attempt an external
mutation.

Its protocol is conceptually:

```python
class EffectAdapter(Protocol):
    def execute(
        self,
        *,
        execution_attempt: ExecutionAttempt,
        effect: RequestedEffect,
        capability: ExecutionCapability,
    ) -> AdapterExecutionResult:
        ...
```

The adapter receives:

- the exact `ExecutionAttempt`;
- the effect selected by custody; and
- the validated `ExecutionCapability`.

Its job is to carry out the external operation and return an observation about
that request.

The adapter does not decide whether the Consequence should be permitted. That
Decision has already been made before custody.

It also does not decide the final Effect. Establishing external reality belongs
to verification.

---

## AdapterExecutionResult Is Not Effect Truth

The adapter returns an `AdapterExecutionResult`.

Its states are:

```text
ACKNOWLEDGED
REJECTED
ERROR
UNKNOWN
```

These states describe what the adapter observed about the execution request.

They do not establish the resulting business Effect.

For example:

```text
ACKNOWLEDGED != BOUND
REJECTED     != NO_BIND
ERROR        != NO_BIND
UNKNOWN      != EFFECT_UNKNOWN
```

An `ACKNOWLEDGED` response may only mean that the external system accepted the
request.

It does not prove that the intended change became effective.

Likewise, an adapter may report `ERROR` even when later verification shows that
the external mutation actually completed.

This is why CAGE keeps execution observation and Effect verification separate.

---

## Explicit Custody Orchestration

`execute_under_custody()` coordinates the controlled execution path.

```text
ExecutionAttempt
      |
      v
Decision
      |
      v
select_effect_for_custody()
      |
      v
validate_capability_for_custody()
      |
      v
EffectAdapter.execute(
    execution_attempt=...,
    effect=selected_effect,
    capability=...
)
      |
      v
AdapterExecutionResult
```

The custody path enforces several rules before and during execution:

- only `ADMITTED` and `NARROWED` Decisions can reach the adapter;
- `NARROWED` sends only `permitted_effect`;
- a capability mismatch stops execution before adapter invocation;
- the adapter must return an `AdapterExecutionResult`;
- the returned result must belong to the expected ExecutionAttempt; and
- custody execution does not create an Effect.

The output of this stage is an execution observation.

Effect establishment happens later, through verification.

---

## EffectVerifier

CAGE separates external execution from external-state verification.

The verification interface is conceptually:

```python
class EffectVerifier(Protocol):
    def verify(
        self,
        *,
        adapter_result: AdapterExecutionResult,
    ) -> EffectVerificationResult:
        ...
```

The roles are different:

```text
EffectAdapter
    -> attempts the external mutation

EffectVerifier
    -> examines what became true afterward
```

The two responsibilities may be implemented by different components.

What matters is that the adapter's observation is not treated as independent
proof of the resulting Effect.

---

## EffectVerificationResult

Verification produces one of three states:

```text
VERIFIED_BOUND
VERIFIED_NO_BIND
INCONCLUSIVE
```

The two conclusive states require supporting verification references.

`INCONCLUSIVE` is used when the available evidence is not enough to establish
either outcome.

The Effect mapping is:

```text
VERIFIED_BOUND
    -> BOUND

VERIFIED_NO_BIND
    -> NO_BIND

INCONCLUSIVE
    -> EFFECT_UNKNOWN
```

An Effect is created from an `EffectVerificationResult` through:

```text
create_effect_from_verification()
```

There is no direct shortcut from:

```text
AdapterExecutionResult
```

to:

```text
Effect
```

The execution observation and the verified Effect represent different facts.

---

## BOUND

`BOUND` means authoritative verification supports the conclusion that the
permitted business Consequence became effective.

It cannot be inferred simply because:

- a command was submitted;
- an API returned success;
- a request was accepted;
- transport completed successfully; or
- no error was reported.

Those signals may be useful evidence, but they are not automatically proof of
the business Effect.

The verification basis must support the `BOUND` claim itself.

---

## NO_BIND

`NO_BIND` means authoritative verification supports the conclusion that the
intended Consequence did not become effective.

It is a stronger claim than saying execution failed or that CAGE did not receive
a useful acknowledgement.

The following are not enough by themselves:

- execution was never attempted;
- execution returned an error;
- the request timed out;
- the connection failed;
- the response was lost;
- the adapter crashed; or
- an acknowledgement was missing.

`VERIFIED_NO_BIND` therefore requires verification references.

Failure describes the execution path.

`NO_BIND` describes verified external reality.

---

## EFFECT_UNKNOWN

When CAGE cannot establish what happened in the external system, the Effect is:

```text
EFFECT_UNKNOWN
```

For example:

```text
execution request sent
        |
        v
adapter observation = ACKNOWLEDGED
        |
        v
authoritative verification = INCONCLUSIVE
        |
        v
EFFECT_UNKNOWN
```

The request may have been accepted, but the available verification evidence is
still insufficient to establish either `BOUND` or `NO_BIND`.

CAGE keeps that uncertainty explicit rather than turning an incomplete
observation into a stronger assurance claim.

---

## Reconciliation

Reconciliation gives CAGE another opportunity to examine external reality for
an execution that has already occurred.

The flow is:

```text
existing AdapterExecutionResult
        |
        v
EffectVerifier
        |
        v
new EffectVerificationResult
```

`reconcile_effect_verification()` receives the existing
`AdapterExecutionResult`.

It does not receive an `EffectAdapter`.

That distinction matters:

```text
reconciliation != replay
reconciliation != execution retry
reconciliation != re-execution
```

Reconciliation observes an existing execution again.

It does not send the operation again.

For example:

```text
one external execution
        |
        v
AdapterExecutionResult #1
        |
        v
Verification #1 = INCONCLUSIVE
        |
        v
Effect #1 = EFFECT_UNKNOWN
        |
        v
Warrant #1
        |
        v
later reconciliation
        |
        v
Verification #2 = VERIFIED_BOUND
        |
        v
Effect #2 = BOUND
        |
        v
Warrant #2
```

The second Warrant can link to the first through:

```text
previous_warrant_id = first_warrant.warrant_id
```

The Consequence, evaluation Attempt, ExecutionAttempt, and
AdapterExecutionResult remain part of the same lineage.

What changed is the evidence available to CAGE.

The external operation was not executed again.

---

## Replay vs Execution Retry vs Reconciliation

These operations address different stages of the CAGE lifecycle.

### Replay

Replay means:

```text
re-evaluate the Consequence
```

It creates a new evaluation `Attempt`.

Replay does not execute the external operation.

### Execution Retry

Execution retry means:

```text
attempt external execution again
```

A retry would require a new `ExecutionAttempt` while preserving the same
Consequence.

v0.6 defines the identity model needed to distinguish a retry from replay, but
does not provide general retry orchestration.

### Reconciliation

Reconciliation means:

```text
verify external reality again
```

It uses an existing `AdapterExecutionResult`.

It does not automatically re-evaluate the Consequence or send the operation
again.

The distinction can be summarized as:

```text
Replay
    -> new evaluation

Execution Retry
    -> new execution attempt

Reconciliation
    -> new observation
```

## Consequence-Level Execution Safety

The consequence-level idempotency model introduced in v0.5 remains important
during custody.

The rule is:

```text
same idempotency key
+
equivalent business intent
=
same Consequence
```

Reusing the same key for materially different business intent produces an
idempotency conflict.

Custody preserves that Consequence identity across:

```text
Decision
ExecutionAttempt
AdapterExecutionResult
EffectVerificationResult
Effect
EffectProof
Warrant
```

Transport retries, provider retries, or repeated API calls must not silently
create a new business Consequence.

The business operation remains the same even when later lifecycle events
receive new identities.

---

## EffectProof

`EffectProof` binds an Effect to the verification that supports it.

It contains:

```text
proof_id
effect
verification
```

where `verification` is an `EffectVerificationResult`.

The proof checks three things.

### Same Consequence

```text
Effect.consequence
==
EffectVerificationResult.consequence
```

### Matching verification and Effect state

```text
VERIFIED_BOUND    <-> BOUND
VERIFIED_NO_BIND  <-> NO_BIND
INCONCLUSIVE      <-> EFFECT_UNKNOWN
```

### Matching verification evidence

```text
Effect.verification_refs
==
EffectVerificationResult.references
```

It also derives:

```text
verification_id
adapter_result_id
execution_attempt_id
```

This keeps the Effect claim tied to the verification lineage that justifies it.

An `EffectProof` is therefore more than a label on an Effect. It preserves the
path back to the observation and verification that support the claim.

---

## Warrant Evolution

A Warrant can exist before external Effect verification.

A decision-only Warrant looks like:

```text
Decision
    |
    v
DecisionProof
    |
    v
Warrant(effect_proof=None)
```

After authoritative verification:

```text
DecisionProof
    +
EffectProof
    |
    v
Warrant
```

When an `EffectProof` is present, the Warrant also derives:

```text
execution_attempt_id
adapter_result_id
verification_id
```

Later reconciliation can produce another Warrant linked through:

```text
previous_warrant_id
```

This lets CAGE extend assurance history without rewriting earlier records.

---

## Historical Integrity

A later verification result does not invalidate what CAGE knew earlier.

For example:

```text
Warrant 1
Effect = EFFECT_UNKNOWN
```

may later be followed by:

```text
Warrant 2
Effect = BOUND
previous_warrant_id = Warrant 1
```

`Warrant 1` remains a valid record of the uncertainty that existed at that
time.

`Warrant 2` records the later assurance state after stronger evidence became
available.

The history keeps both views rather than rewriting the earlier one.

## v0.6 Non-Negotiable Invariants

### Invariant 1 — Decision != Effect

A Decision is not evidence of external reality.

### Invariant 2 — ADMITTED does not automatically execute

Evaluation and external execution remain separate operations.

### Invariant 3 — Execution requires explicit custody

External mutation occurs only through an explicit custody path.

### Invariant 4 — Replay does not execute

Replay is an evaluation operation.

### Invariant 5 — ExecutionAttempt != evaluation Attempt

They represent different lifecycle events and have separate identities.

### Invariant 6 — AdapterExecutionResult != Effect

Adapter observations cannot establish `BOUND`, `NO_BIND`, or
`EFFECT_UNKNOWN`.

### Invariant 7 — Uncertainty remains explicit

When external reality cannot be established, the Effect remains
`EFFECT_UNKNOWN`.

### Invariant 8 — Consequence identity survives custody

Execution and verification lineage remain tied to the original Consequence.

### Invariant 9 — NARROWED executes only permitted_effect

The broader original RequestedEffect must not reach the adapter.

### Invariant 10 — ExecutionCapability is scoped

The capability must match the intended Consequence, action type, and Resource.

### Invariant 11 — Effect claims require authoritative verification

An Effect is created only from verification-backed evidence.

### Invariant 12 — NO_BIND requires evidence of non-effect

Failure, timeout, rejection, or missing acknowledgement is not enough.

### Invariant 13 — Reconciliation does not execute

Reconciliation observes an existing execution result and does not invoke an
EffectAdapter.

### Invariant 14 — Custody does not become IAM

CAGE models execution-authority scope without becoming a credential or policy
system.

### Invariant 15 — Core remains vendor-neutral

Provider-specific cloud, database, payment, or identity semantics stay outside
the generic Core.

### Invariant 16 — Historical assurance remains preserved

Later evidence extends the history without rewriting earlier Warrants.

The implemented custody lineage can be summarized as:

```text
